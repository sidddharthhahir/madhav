Question
I keep losing my temper with the people I love most. What do I do?

The Gītā does not treat anger as a character flaw to be ashamed of. It treats it as a mechanism, and it names the part that comes first: desire. BG 3.37 calls desire and anger a single thing seen at two moments — the same heat, first reaching outward, then striking. Krishna's word for it is vairin: the enemy stationed inside.

Which reframes what you are asking. You are not trying to become a person who never feels the heat. You are trying to stop being carried by it. The instruction in BG 2.47 is usually read as a lesson about work, but it applies exactly here: act, and release the claim on how the other person responds. Most flashes of temper at home are a claim being denied — they should have listened, they should have noticed.

Then BG 6.5 gives you the practical half, and it is blunt about where the work sits: the self is lifted by the self, and the self alone is the self's enemy. No one is coming to regulate this for you. In practice that is small and unspectacular — noticing the heat one beat earlier each time, and leaving the room while leaving is still a choice.

One caution the text is clear about: BG 16.18 puts anger in company with pride and self-importance — the family of states that make other people into obstacles. If the people you love are starting to look like obstacles, that is the thing to work on, not the volume of your voice.
